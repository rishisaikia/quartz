## Definition
Approaching a problem backward by considering what you want to avoid rather than what you want to achieve.

## Key Insight
It's often easier to identify what causes failure than what guarantees success. By inverting the problem—asking "How could I make this worse?"—you uncover hidden risks and create better solutions.

## How to Apply
Instead of asking "How do I succeed?" ask:
- "How could I guarantee failure?"
- "What would make this terrible?"
- Then avoid doing those things

## Real-World Example
Instead of asking "How do I have a happy marriage?" you could ask "How would I ruin my marriage?" Answers: Never communicate, always criticize, keep score of who does more, never apologize, prioritize work over family. Now you have a clear list of behaviors to avoid, which is often more actionable than vague advice to "communicate better."

## Common Pitfalls
- Becoming too negative and risk-averse
- Focusing only on avoiding failure without pursuing success
- Creating fear-based decision making
