## Definition
People respond to incentives—the rewards and punishments that shape behavior. Understanding what people are incentivized to do reveals what they will actually do.

## Key Insight
"Show me the incentive and I'll show you the outcome." People aren't good or bad, rational or irrational—they respond to the incentive structures they face. If you want to predict or change behavior, look at the incentives.

## How to Apply
When analyzing any system or person's behavior:
- What are they rewarded for?
- What are they punished for?
- Are incentives aligned with stated goals?
- What unintended behaviors do current incentives encourage?

## Real-World Example
A software company pays developers based on lines of code written. Developers respond to this incentive by writing bloated, unnecessarily complex code. The company wanted productivity but incentivized quantity over quality.

A sales team is compensated purely on new customer acquisition. They respond by over-promising to close deals, creating angry customers and overwhelming the service team. The company wanted growth but incentivized short-term gains over customer satisfaction.

In colonial India, the British government wanted to reduce cobra populations and offered a bounty for dead cobras. People responded by breeding cobras to kill for the bounty. When the program ended, breeders released their cobras, making the problem worse.

## Common Pitfalls
- Assuming people's incentives match what they say
- Creating incentives that reward the metric, not the goal
- Underestimating how creative people are at gaming incentives
