## Definition
Roughly 80% of effects come from 20% of causes. A small number of inputs often create the majority of outputs.

## Key Insight
Not all effort is equal. A minority of your actions, relationships, and possessions create the majority of your results and satisfaction. Identifying and focusing on that vital 20% multiplies your effectiveness.

## How to Apply
Regularly audit your life and work:
- Which 20% of activities produce 80% of your results?
- Which 20% of customers generate 80% of revenue?
- Which 20% of your clothes do you wear 80% of the time?

Focus energy on the vital few, eliminate or delegate the trivial many.

## Real-World Example
A software company analyzes customer complaints and discovers that 80% of support tickets come from just 3 features out of 50. By fixing those 3 features, they drastically reduce support burden. Similarly, they find that 20% of their features drive 80% of user engagement, so they focus development there instead of building more features nobody uses.

## Common Pitfalls
- Taking the 80/20 split too literally (it's illustrative, not exact)
- Neglecting necessary maintenance on the "80%"
- Ignoring potential high-value activities not yet in your routine
