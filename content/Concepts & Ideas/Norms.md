We use signals to compete. Signals lead to some spectacular results (art, science, music, etc.), but left unrestrained, it leads to an arms-race which is wasteful, and would hold the entire species back. 

*Why Animals can't stop the arms race:*
Animals and plants can't stop this arms race because of their inability to look ahead. Even if redwoods were to be able to decide on a height cap, all it would take is for a single mutation to be present, and they'll grab all the sunlight, at the cost of all the other trees. This will ensure that the next generation of redwoods would be even more rivalrous. Eventually, all the trees will all be back to being as tall as before. Hence, redwoods have no way out to stop this arms-race for the good of the species. 

*How Humans cooperate to stop the arms race:*
Unlike redwoods, humans are able to look ahead and coordinate, turning wasteful expenditure into productive cooperation. By way of rules or standards (i.e. Norms) about how individuals should behave in a community, humans avoid an arms race. They are defined by what benefits majority of the people in the group. They are collectively enforced and violations are subject to punishments which vary basis severity of norm being violated. 

*Origin of Norms:*
In forager societies, humans were extremely reliant on each other, as being outside a group was like a death sentence. Foragers had a very egalitarian society, with each man, woman or child having the same work to perform. They had norms to ensure that no individual or coalition was dominant and the group was paramount. They had norms to ensure that no individual or coalition became dominant. These norms are deeply embedded in us even today. 

As our civilisation evolved, so did our norms. With agriculture, we evolved the concepts of property, marriage and war; consequently, to enforce these, we also evolved stronger norms to ensure social conformity.

*Enforcement of Norms*
Unlike in animals that shirk away from picking a fight with another due to fear of being overpowered, or those that share food with the hope of reciprocity, humans confirm to norms due to fear of reprisal from third parties as well. This collective enforcement is the essence of norms. 

Norms can be difficult to enforce. In modern society they are enforced by institutions like law, court and police. But in social circles, they are enforced by [[gossip]] and [[reputation]]. Since sex, status and political success depends on our reputation, gossip is an effective mechanism to check their behaviour. In that sense, gossip and reputation incentivise people to follow norms.

Subtle norms include bragging, currying favour, subgroup politics, selfish motives.

So norms help curb competition, and in that sense, should also hinder growth of intelligence. But it doesn't, because continuously indulge in [[Cheating on Norms]]. This push/ pull is what leads to growth of intelligence.
