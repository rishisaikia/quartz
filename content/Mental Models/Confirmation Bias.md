## Definition

The tendency to search for, interpret, and remember information in a way that confirms your existing beliefs while ignoring or dismissing contradictory evidence.

## Key Insight

We don't naturally seek truth—we seek confirmation. Your brain automatically filters reality to match what you already believe, making you blind to alternative explanations and evidence.

## How to Apply

Actively seek disconfirming evidence:
- Before making a decision, ask "What would prove me wrong?"
- Seek out people who disagree with you
- Ask "What would I need to see to change my mind?"
- Keep a record of predictions to check your accuracy

## Real-World Example

You believe you're a good judge of character and hire someone who seems impressive in interviews. Over months, they underperform—missing deadlines, poor quality work. But you remember the great interview and ignore red flags: "They're just adjusting" or "They had a bad week." You only notice evidence supporting your initial positive impression until the situation becomes undeniable.

Meanwhile, another employee you weren't initially impressed with does solid work, but you barely notice because you're not looking for it.

## Common Pitfalls

- Thinking you're immune to confirmation bias
- Only seeking diverse opinions that still confirm your views
- Becoming cynical and trusting nothing

## Notes

**Evolutionary basis**: Confirmation bias may have helped maintain group cohesion and shared belief systems, which were crucial for cooperation and survival in small tribal groups.
