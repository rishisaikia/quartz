## Key Insight
We use laughter as a play signal; we use it to reassure that a particular situation is safe, specifically when there's an element of danger involved. When we laugh at ourselves, we are signalling to others that our intentions are ultimately playful. When we laugh in response to someone else's actions, we are saying that we perceive their actions as playful. 
- We laugh far more in social settings than when we are alone (which is why laugh tracks work). 
- Laugh is vocal, and throughout the animal world, vocalisation = active communication. Even kids seem to laugh to communication; for example, kids laugh more when tickled by mothers than by strangers - they use it as a message to regulate a social interaction.
- Speakers laugh more than listeners
- All 5 great apes laugh.

All of these suggest that laughter isn't a knee-jerk reaction, but something that serves a specific biological function.

Two variables determine if we can laugh at a tragedy. 
1. Amount of pain involved
2. We are more likely to laugh at the misfortune of those we are psychologically distant from. 

Laughter has two qualities:
1. In Risu Veritas - Laughter is an honest signal
2. It gives us a way out of situations

## Real-World Examples
We don't laugh continuously. We only feel the need for 'reassurance' in situations that, if not for the laughter, can be interpreted as too serious or too dangerous. This explains why when we are kids, we laugh in situations of mock aggresion (tickling), mock danger (being thrown in the air) and surprises (peekaboo). As we grow up, these adventures turn into explorations of [[Social Norms as a Means to Regulate Signaling]].
