## Definition
A system's tendency to maintain internal stability by adjusting to changes in external conditions. Systems resist change and work to return to equilibrium.

## Key Insight
Living systems and organizations naturally resist disruption and work to restore balance. This is useful for stability but makes change difficult. Understanding homeostasis explains why transformation efforts often fail—systems fight back against change.

## How to Apply
When trying to change a system:
- Expect resistance (it's natural, not personal)
- Change multiple reinforcing factors simultaneously
- Maintain pressure through the adjustment period
- Understand what's trying to restore the old equilibrium

When designing systems:
- Build in healthy homeostatic mechanisms
- Know when homeostasis is maintaining dysfunction

## Real-World Example
Your body maintains temperature around 98.6°F through homeostasis. Too hot? You sweat. Too cold? You shiver. External changes trigger internal adjustments to restore equilibrium. This keeps you alive but also resists change (why dieting is hard—your body fights to return to its previous weight).

A company tries to change its culture to be more innovative. Employees have learned the old rules ("don't rock the boat"), and organizational systems (performance reviews, promotion criteria, meeting structures) all reinforce the old culture. Every new behavior triggers homeostatic resistance pulling back to the familiar equilibrium.

You try to wake up earlier. For a week, you manage 6am wake-ups. But your evening habits haven't changed—you still stay up late. Your sleep schedule fights back (homeostasis), making you exhausted. Eventually you sleep in again, returning to equilibrium.

Ecosystems maintain homeostatic balance. Remove wolves, deer population explodes, vegetation is overgrazed, ecosystem degrades until a new (often worse) equilibrium is found.

## Common Pitfalls
- Giving up when the system resists (expecting change to be easy)
- Not identifying all the factors maintaining the current equilibrium
- Fighting homeostasis in systems where stability is actually valuable
