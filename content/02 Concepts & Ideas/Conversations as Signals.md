## Key Insight
On the surface, conversations seem like an exercise in information exchange. But they are in fact a way for speakers to show off their wit, perception, status and intelligence, while it's a way for listeners to find speakers they would want to associate with. 

You incur costs when you speak in a conversation. The costs include (i) cost of gathering the information you communicate, and (ii) the cost of sharing valuable information that you could have kept to yourself. Yet, people freely indulge in conversation despite these costs. What do they get in return? One school of thought is that conversation is a mutual give and take of information, a reciprocal act. However, this doesn't seem correct: 
1. We know that people are generally more eager to speak. If conversation was as much about receiving information as communicating, people would listen as much 
2. People don't keep track of conversational debt to enforce reciprocity. 
3. Conversations needn't be about relevant topics only; in fact, could be a list of disconnected topics that maximises the information exchange
4. There would be no sub-optimal conversations or 'small talk', and people would always cut to the chase. 

The payoff for speaking in a conversation isn't information, but status. Essentially, when you are speaking, you are showing off with the hope of impressing and winning over allies and mates. Speakers in conversations get evaluated not for the specific information they are sharing there and then (trading mentality) but for the full extent of their knowledge (alliance mentality). In other words, every remark from a speaker has two messages: *text* and *subtext*. *Text* is the information, which is important sometimes, but very often it's actually the *subtext* that interests you. As in the case of an interview, you are not trying to learn new things from the interviewee about a specific domain, but trying to guage them as a potential coworker/ ally. 

## Real-World Examples
Why do we follow the news? If it was about gathering useful information:
1. We'd be more careful about the sources of news and their accuracy
2. We consume much higher quantity of news than is practically useful for us
We follow the news so that we can use them to fuel our conversations and appear impressive to others. We maintain a list of few hot topics that we like to stay on top off for this purpose.
