We have been taught to look at our senses as a mirror of the external world, as if we create a copy of what we see in our minds. Hence, the detail and consistency that is there in the external world, would also find replication via our senses. But this doesn't seem to be the case at all and we now know that perception requires *inference*. 

### Inconsistency of our perception:
Impossible objects tell us three things:
*Mind as Mirror*: The mind as mirror metaphor cannot be right since we cannot hold an accurate representation of an impossible object in our minds. 
*Perceiving in Instalments*: We grasp different parts of an object at different times. Since scanning various parts of impossible objects makes us feel it's 3D and 2D, we perceive objects in parts. ^65f309
*Confidence*: When looking at an impossible object, we have confidence that we're perceiving a 3D object; however, this confidence is misplaced because an impossible object cannot exist in the real world.  ^82a267

### Sparseness of our perception:
Our eyes are constructed in such a way that only a small area around the [[Brain Captures Knowledge in Reference Frames#^ce74dc|fovea has enough sensitivity to perceive both granular detail and rich colour]]. Anything beyond this area is a gray fuzz; however, our mind tricks us into thinking that the entire visual spectrum is steady and available to us in rich detail. One way to look beyond this trick and realise the narrow focus of our eyes is the following optical illusion where the dark spots seem to appear only in the areas where we are directing our gaze.  ^a9fb96

Further, our eyes move rapidly in [[Brain Captures Knowledge in Reference Frames#^06dbc1|saccades every second, yet we perceive the images to be stable]]. Our eyes jump from point to point on a scene, and stitch together an illusion of rich visual world. This jumping or *jitter* is partly unconscious. Retinal stabilisation helps look beyond this.
