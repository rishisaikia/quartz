## Definition
Breaking down complex problems into their most basic, foundational truths and reasoning up from there, rather than reasoning by analogy.

## Key Insight
Most of our thinking relies on what others have done before. First principles thinking means questioning every assumption until you reach fundamental truths that cannot be reduced further.

## How to Apply
1. Identify your current assumptions about a problem
2. Break them down by asking "Why?" and "Is this necessarily true?"
3. Reach the foundational truths
4. Reason upward to create new solutions

## Real-World Example
Elon Musk wanted to build rockets but they cost $65 million each. Instead of accepting this, he asked: "What are rockets made of? Aerospace-grade aluminum, titanium, copper, carbon fiber." He calculated the raw materials cost only about 2% of the rocket price. By building rockets from first principles, SpaceX reduced costs dramatically and made space travel more accessible.

## Common Pitfalls
- Going too deep and wasting time on truly fundamental philosophical questions
- Ignoring valuable knowledge that doesn't need reinventing
- Underestimating the complexity of implementation
