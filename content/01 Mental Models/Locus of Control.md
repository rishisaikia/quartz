## Definition
The degree to which you believe you have control over the outcomes in your life. Internal locus: outcomes are determined by your actions. External locus: outcomes are determined by outside forces (luck, fate, other people).

## Key Insight
Your locus of control shapes how you respond to everything. Internal locus drives action and resilience—"I can influence this." External locus drives passivity—"It's out of my hands." Neither extreme is accurate, but internal bias generally leads to better outcomes because you try to change what you can.

## How to Apply
Assess your locus of control:
- Do you attribute success to your effort or to luck/circumstances?
- Do you attribute failure to your mistakes or to external factors?
- When things go wrong, do you ask "What can I do?" or "Why does this happen to me?"

Shift toward internal locus (selectively):
- Focus on what you can control
- Take responsibility for your responses
- But accept what you truly can't change

## Real-World Example
Two employees don't get promoted:
- **External locus**: "My boss has favorites. The system is rigged. No point trying." They become passive, bitter, and stagnant.
- **Internal locus**: "What skills do I need? Who should I talk to? What can I do better?" They get feedback, improve, and eventually advance.

Same situation, different mindsets, completely different outcomes.

Health: External locus—"Genetics determine health, nothing I can do." Eats poorly, doesn't exercise. Internal locus—"My choices matter. I can improve my health." Eats well, exercises, gets checkups. The first person is right that genetics matter, but their external locus makes them helpless.

Dating: External locus—"I'm unlucky in love. Dating apps don't work for me." Internal locus—"What can I improve? Better photos? More interesting profile? Different approach?" One gives up, one experiments and improves.

## Common Pitfalls
- Extreme internal locus (blaming yourself for everything, including things outside your control)
- Extreme external locus (taking no responsibility for anything)
- Not distinguishing between what you can and can't control
- Using external locus to avoid responsibility or internal locus to carry false guilt
