
Purpose of sex has been considered the queen of evolutionary problems. 

When Darwin first came up with Evolution in mid-19th century, it had become very popular as people had misunderstood its central message as a theory of steady progress, a ladder of self-improvement.

Earlier assumption was that sex was the only way to reproduce/ have babies. Hence, that was its purpose. Later, it was discovered there are many better ways to reproduce:
- Dandelions produce seeds that are clones of themselves
- Virgin greenflies give birth to virgin young that are already pregnant with their own virgins
- Willow trees grow from cuttings

**What Genetic Purpose does Sex Serve?**
Meiosis is the process by which parents select the genes that will go into reproduction. During this process, the 23 pairs of chromosomes are laid alongside their opposite number, and chunks of one set are swapped with chunks of the other. This is *recombination* and what it results in is the eventual baby will get a thorough mixture of its four grandparents. 

After recombination, one whole set is then passed on to the offspring and married off to the whole set of the other parent. This process is called *outcrossing*, and this ensures that the baby has both the parents' genes. 

Thus, from the point of view of above, sex can be deemed to be more about a creature borrowings another's genes than reproduction itself, similar to the way bacteria do it. This mixing of genes leads to more variety, which eventually leads to evolution. But its not clear why this is a good thing. Some theories:

*Sex leads to Evolution; evolution is an imperative.*
Teleological Explanation - Vicar of Bray. Genetic mixing is important because it leads to variety from which natural selection can choose. Sex leads to novel mixtures in genes that leads to new inventions, from which evolution can pick winners (survivors).

The problem with above is that it assumes evolution to be an imperative - as if evolution were a goal that every species is striving for. But this teleological explanation doesn't make sense because evolution is a directionless process that sometimes even makes an animal's descendants more complicated, sometimes simpler, and sometimes doesn't effect any changes at all. Evolution is not a goal, but a means to solving a problem.

*Evolution might not be end goal, but does advantage sexual species:*
Evolution might not be the end-goal or pre-ordained, but was at least essential. Sexual species operate like open source, freely sharing their newly invented genes with other individuals, so that the entire species can take advantage. Asexual species, on the other hand, do not share knowledge. This puts them at a disadvantage. So while evolution might not be pre-ordained, sexual creatures are definitely at an advantage. This theory was taken further by mathematically demonstrating that rare mutations can come together in sexual species, but not in asexual species - sexual species do not have to wait for two rare events (mutataions) to occur in the same individual, but can combine them from others. 

Individual Incentive:
People often misinterpret evolution as inter-species struggle to survive. Yet, the competition is mainly within the same species. When the fittest are struggling to survive, with whom are they competing? With other members of the same species. A gazelle in Africa isn't just trying to outrun cheetah, but also other gazelles. Similarly, people don't just use their intellect to solve practical problems, but to outwith each other, deceive people, deceit, understand people's motives, manipulate others, etc. So what matters is not how clever you are, but how much *more* clever than other people. Oviously, the best an individual can do for its species is to survive and breed; however, often the survival of the individual and what is good for the species is in conflict. So natural selection is not going to pick genes that help gazelles survive as a species but hurt the chances of individuals - because such genes will be wiped out long before they can show any benefits. So in the absence of altruism, what sex needs is a strong motivation for the individual.

Individual Incentive for sex:
The explanation for sex was still about the good of the species. So there is still no incentive for an individual to have sex, unless motivated by altruistic motives. So how could a gene for sexual reproduction spread at the expense of an asexual gene? How could a sexual creature leave behind more descendants than an asexual creature? Mathematically, asexual creatures will always spread faster than sexual ones since in latter, males do not reproduce - this is called the *cost of meiosis* or *cost of males*. But we know that there must be SOME advantage that accrues to the individual since every mammal and bird, most invertebrate animals and most plants and fungi are sexual. 

Dilemma:
Sex serves the species at the cost of the individual. Selfish individuals could easily abandon sex and outcompete their sexual rivals. But they don't do this. Hence, sex must have some mysterious incentive for the INDIVIDUAL. 

*Gene Repair Theory*
The machinery for sex is same or has evolved from the machinery for gene repair. DNA is a long, thin molecule that is made up of 4 chemicals (ATCG). Each letter is complementary to another, i.e. each prefers to align itself opposite another specific letter. Thus, A pairs with T and C with G. This means there is an automatic way of copying DNA: by going along the strand of molecule, stitching together another from the complementary letters. E.g. AAGTTC becomes TTCAAG. Copy this, and you get the original sequence back. Every gene contains a strand of DNA and its complementary copy entwined in a double helix. Specia enzymes move up and down the strands and where they find a break, repair it by referencing the complementary strand. But when both strands are damaged at same place, the repair enzymes need a template. Sex provides this by introducing a copy of the same gene from another creature via outcrossing or from another chromosome via recombination in the same creature. 
 But if sex is just about getting gene copies for repair, isn't it better to get them from relatives than from strangers?

*Ratchet or Mutation*

Children have 100 differences from parents as a result of random copying errors by enzymes or as a result of mutations in parents' ovaries or testicles by cosmic rays. Of these, 99 wouldn't matter as they'll be silent or neutral mutations. But steady accumulation of defects. 

Traditionally, mutations have been considered bad as a lot of them kill their owners or inheritors. But occasionally, a genuine improvement occurs. 

For a long time, geneticists focused on these good mutations and considered sex as a way of distributing them among the population, like cross-fertilisation of good ideas in universities and industries. Sex constantly rearranges genes into new combinations, until luckily synergy results. One ancestor giraffe invented long legs, while another invented a long neck. But this confuses consequences for cause. Advantages are far too remote - they will appear after a few generations, and by that time, asexual competitors would have already outpopulated. Besides, if sex is good at putting together good combinations of genes, it will be even better at breaking them up.

Focussing on bad mutations or Muller's Ratchet: The idea is that genetic defects accumulate, and the only way to prevent the ratchet from turning is for perfect flea to have sex and pass its defect-free genes to other fleas before it dies. For e.g. in a tank with 10 water fleas, suppose there is only 1 flea entirely free of mutation and others have 1 or several defects. Further, suppose that on average, only 5 of the water fleas in each generation manage to breed before being eaten by a fish. The defect-free flea has a 1-in-2 chance of not freezing. So does the flea with most defects, but the difference is that once the defect-free flea is dead, the only way to be re-created is for another mutation to correct the mutation in a flea with a defect, which is rare. On the other hand, a flea with two defects can be easily recreated by a single mutation in a water flea with 1 or more defects. In other words, the random loss of certain lines of descent means that the average number of defects gradually increases. Just as a ratchet turns easily one way but cannot be turned back, so genetic defects inevitably accumulate. This is sort of like a photocopy machine that can always churn out bad copies of the original, but difficult to recreate the original once the original is lost.
