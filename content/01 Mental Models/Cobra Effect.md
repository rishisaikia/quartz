## Definition
The cobra effect describes situations where an attempted solution to a problem makes the problem worse by creating incentives that change behavior in unintended ways.

## Key Insight
People respond to incentives, not intentions. When rewards or penalties are poorly designed, rational behavior at the individual level can produce irrational outcomes at the system level.

## How to Apply
For positive outcomes:
- Examine how people might game the rule, not how you want them to behave
- Design incentives around outcomes, not proxies
- Test policies in small, reversible ways before scaling

For negative outcomes:
- Watch for metrics improving while reality worsens
- Be suspicious of sudden compliance spikes
- Remove or redesign incentives that reward surface-level signals

## Real-World Examples
A government offers a bounty for killing cobras to reduce snake population. People begin breeding cobras to kill them for money. When the program ends, breeders release the snakes, increasing the cobra population.

A company rewards employees based on number of emails responded to. Employees reply faster but with lower quality, increasing confusion and follow-up work.

A school ties teacher pay strictly to test scores. Teaching narrows to test prep, while real understanding declines.

## Common Pitfalls
- Assuming people will behave “as intended”
- Confusing correlation with genuine improvement
- Optimizing for easy-to-measure metrics instead of real goals

## Notes
Before introducing any rule, ask: “If someone wanted to exploit this, how would they do it?” The answer often reveals the hidden failure mode.
