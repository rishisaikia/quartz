**Radiocarbon dating** (or **carbon-14 dating**) is a method used to determine the age of ancient objects that were once living, by measuring the amount of **carbon-14** (a radioactive isotope of carbon) remaining in them. 

Here's how it works:

### 1. **The Basics of Carbon-14**:
   - Carbon-14 (\(^{14}\text{C}\)) is a radioactive isotope of carbon that is naturally produced in the Earth's atmosphere when cosmic rays interact with nitrogen.
   - Living organisms, like plants and animals, absorb carbon (both \(^{12}\text{C}\), a stable isotope, and \(^{14}\text{C}\)) from the atmosphere or through their food, maintaining a constant ratio of \(^{12}\text{C}\) to \(^{14}\text{C}\) in their tissues while alive.

### 2. **How Dating Works**:
   - When an organism dies, it stops absorbing carbon, and the \(^{14}\text{C}\) in its body begins to decay into nitrogen-14 (\(^{14}\text{N}\)) at a known, constant rate (its half-life is about 5,730 years).
   - By measuring the amount of \(^{14}\text{C}\) remaining in the sample and comparing it to the initial levels of \(^{14}\text{C}\), scientists can estimate how long it has been since the organism died.

### 3. **Half-Life**:
   The half-life of \(^{14}\text{C}\) is about 5,730 years, meaning that after this time, half of the original \(^{14}\text{C}\) has decayed. This is what makes radiocarbon dating useful for dating objects that are up to about **50,000 years old**. Beyond this range, the remaining \(^{14}\text{C}\) becomes too small to measure accurately.

### 4. **Applications**:
   - Radiocarbon dating is commonly used in **archaeology**, **paleontology**, and **geology** to date ancient organic materials such as wood, bones, charcoal, and textiles.
   - It helps scientists understand the age of fossils, historical artifacts, and even ancient climate patterns.

### 5. **Limitations**:
   - It only works on **organic materials** (things that were once living).
   - It is less accurate for very old samples (beyond ~50,000 years), and contamination can affect the results.
   - It assumes that the level of \(^{14}\text{C}\) in the atmosphere has been constant over time, though this can be calibrated with other dating methods.

In short, radiocarbon dating is a powerful tool for determining the age of formerly living things by measuring the decay of carbon-14.
