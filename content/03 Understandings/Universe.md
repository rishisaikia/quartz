The solar core is dense and pressurised. Due to this, the hydrogen in the core is stripped of its atoms, and the resulting nuclei are mashed together to build heavier nuclei. The resulting nuclei have mass lesser than the sum of the participating nuclei. This extra missing mass converts to energy, which then is released as electromagnetic radiation (and light). This is also the energy that balances the gravity which would have otherwise led the sun to collapse in on itself.

When a sun eventually runs out of this nuclear fuel at the core, the density and pressure at the core is not enough to sustain nuclear fusion. In this scenario, gravity wins, and the core starts shrinking. With this, the pressure and density at the outer shell of the core again starts building up. As a result, nuclear fusion again starts, but this time in the outer shell. The resulting energy this time around is much higher than the gravity of the star, resulting in the star growing in size.

This is going to happen to our Sun in another 4 million years. 

The lifespan of a star is inversely proportional to its size. Large stars burn out their fuel much faster than smaller stars. This is why Red Dwarves live much longer. 

The solar system is made out of 8 planets revolving around the sun in a flat plane. The 4 inner, rocky planets are separated from the outer giants by a belt of asteroids. These are irregular shaped rocks. Every once in a while, one of these rocks collide and fall down to earth, and are called metiorites. 

The 4 gas giants also have a rocky core, but are covered by dense gas. 

Beyond the planets is the Kuiper Belt. Similar to the asteroid belt, but made of icy objects. Similar to the asteroid belt, sometimes one or two get knocked around the starts getting attracted by the sun. When this happens, they start melting, leaving a trail. These are called comets. 

Beyond the Kuiper belt is the Oort Cloud, a dense sphere of large rocks that completely surrounds the solar system. This is apparently theoretical, and no one has actually seen it.

If we assume the solar system ends with the Kuiper belt, then the diameter isn't even 1 light year; however, if we include the Oort Cloud, then the diameter is as large as 3 light years.

The nearest star to the solar system is Proxima Centauri, which is 4 light years away. 

All the stars that we see in the night sky are part of a large collection of stars gravitationally bound to a relatively disc-shaped spiral structure called the Milky Way. At the centre of the Milky Way is a black hole (called Sagittarius A*). A star called S2 (or Source 2) orbits this black hole at a very short distance. It travels extremely fast; till very recently, it was the fastest detected celestial body.

The South Pole of the earth points roughly to the centre of the Milky Way, while the North Pole points away. This is why the night sky in South is much busier than in the North. 

The Milky Way has a diameter of around 100,000 light years, and a thickness of around 1000 light years. It has around 300 billion stars.

The nearest galaxy to the Milky Way is the much larger (has around 1000 billion stars) Andromeda Galaxy, at a distance of around 2.5 million light years. 

Both these galaxies are headed toward each other and will merge in the future.

A collection of 50 odd such galaxies is termed the Local Group.

Similar to the local group, there are other groups of galaxies. Much larger groups of galaxies comprising 100s and 1000s of galaxies are called Clusters. Still larger groups are called super clusters. 

Till after 380000 years after the Big Bang, the universe was very dense. It was made of plasma. It was so dense that light could not travel within it. As it expanded and cooled, it became transparent. This phenomenon is called recombination and place and time at which this happened is called the "Surface of last scattering". Theoretically, we can't see beyond this limit.
