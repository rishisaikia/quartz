## Definition
Growth that accelerates over time because each period's increase builds on the previous total, creating a doubling pattern rather than linear addition.

## Key Insight
Human intuition is linear—we think in straight lines. But many important phenomena grow exponentially. The difference between linear and exponential growth seems small at first, then becomes dramatic. This makes exponential growth both dangerous (debts, epidemics) and powerful (investments, learning).

## How to Apply
Identify what's growing exponentially:
- Does each unit create more units? (viral spread, compound returns)
- Are we in the early, slow phase or the explosive phase?
- For threats: Act early while growth is manageable
- For opportunities: Be patient through the slow early phase

## Real-World Example
A pond has one lily pad. Each day, the lily pads double. On day 30, the pond is fully covered. On what day was the pond half covered? Day 29. This is exponential growth—it seems slow for a long time (nearly empty on day 28), then suddenly explosive.

COVID-19 illustrated this: going from 100 cases to 200 to 400 looked manageable. But the same doubling rate meant going from 10,000 to 20,000 to 40,000 very quickly. Linear thinkers see "just 100 more cases today" while exponential thinkers see "the doubling rate is still 3 days—we're in trouble."

The folktale of the chessboard: If you place one grain of rice on square 1, two on square 2, four on square 3, doubling each time, by square 64 you'd have more rice than exists on Earth.

## Common Pitfalls
- Underestimating exponential growth in early stages
- Expecting all growth to be exponential (most isn't)
- Not recognizing when exponential growth must eventually plateau
