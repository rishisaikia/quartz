## Definition

>Past decisions constrain future options. History matters.

The path you've taken determines which paths are available next, even if a different route would be better. 

## Key Insight

The best choice today isn't always available—it depends on choices made yesterday. Once you're on a path, switching costs and momentum keep you there even if it's suboptimal. This explains why obviously better solutions don't get adopted: we're stuck on a path that made sense at the time but is now constraining.

## How to Apply

Recognize path dependence:
- "Why do we still do it this way?" → Often because of past decisions
- Early choices have outsized impact on long-term outcomes
- Sometimes the best move is to backtrack and start over

Make decisions considering:
- "How will this constrain future options?"
- "Am I optimizing for now or for flexibility later?"
- "What paths am I closing off?"

## Real-World Example

**QWERTY keyboard**: Designed in the 1870s to prevent mechanical typewriter jams by placing common letter pairs apart. Typewriters don't jam anymore, but we're stuck with QWERTY because billions of people learned it, all keyboards use it, and switching would be massively disruptive. Better layouts exist (Dvorak, Colemak) but path dependence keeps us on QWERTY.

**City layouts**: Why are streets in old European cities winding and confusing while American cities have grids? European cities were built for walking and horses, then had cars added later. You can't rebuild the whole city, so you're stuck with the old layout. American cities were built for cars from the start.

**Your career**: Early career choices limit later options. If you spent 10 years in finance, switching to medicine requires starting over. The path you're on makes certain moves easier (senior finance roles) and others harder (unrelated fields). Early choices compound.

**VHS vs Betamax**: Betamax was technically superior, but VHS got early market share, which led to more movies on VHS, which led to more people buying VHS players, which led to more movies on VHS... Path dependence created a winner even though the better technology lost.

## Common Pitfalls

- Not recognizing when you're constrained by past decisions
- Continuing down a bad path because you've invested in it ([[Sunk Cost Fallacy]])
- Not making early decisions with future path dependence in mind
- Assuming the current path is optimal just because it's established
