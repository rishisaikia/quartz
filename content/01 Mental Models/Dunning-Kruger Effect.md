## Definition
Cognitive bias where people with low competence in a domain overestimate their ability, while experts tend to underestimate theirs. Incompetence often comes with unwarranted confidence.

## Key Insight
The knowledge required to be good at something is often the same knowledge required to recognize you're not good at it. Beginners don't know what they don't know, creating false confidence. Experts see the complexity others miss, creating doubt.

## How to Apply
Recognize the pattern:
- High confidence + low experience = danger zone
- High confidence + high experience = possible expertise
- Low confidence + high experience = likely expert being humble
- When you feel most confident, ask "What am I missing?"

## Real-World Example
A person takes a weekend photography workshop and suddenly believes they could charge professional rates, criticizing experienced photographers' work. They don't yet understand lighting, composition rules, post-processing, client management, or the business side—so they don't know these gaps exist.

Meanwhile, a photographer with 20 years of experience constantly sees flaws in their own work and hesitates to call themselves an expert because they understand how deep the craft goes.

This is why cryptocurrency Twitter was full of "experts" during the bull run—people who'd been investing for 3 months explaining markets to Warren Buffett.

## Common Pitfalls
- Using this to dismiss all confident people
- Assuming all humble people are experts
- Not recognizing it in yourself (meta-Dunning-Kruger)
