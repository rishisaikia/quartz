## Definition
The value of the next best alternative you give up when making a choice.

## Key Insight
Every decision carries a hidden cost—not just what you pay, but what you could have gained by choosing differently. Time and attention have opportunity costs just like money.

## How to Apply
Before committing resources (time, money, attention), ask: "What else could I do with these resources?" Compare the value of alternatives, not just the immediate option.

## Real-World Example
You have $10,000 and a free Saturday. You could:
- Spend the money on a vacation and the Saturday relaxing
- Invest the money (potentially growing to $15,000 in 5 years) and spend Saturday learning a new skill

Choosing the vacation means the opportunity cost is both the investment returns AND the skill development. The vacation needs to be worth more to you than those combined benefits.

## Common Pitfalls
- Overanalyzing small decisions (decision paralysis)
- Forgetting that doing nothing is also a choice with opportunity costs
- Only considering financial costs, ignoring time/energy/happiness
