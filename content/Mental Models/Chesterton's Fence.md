## Definition
Don't remove a fence, change a rule, or eliminate a practice until you understand why it was built in the first place. Reforms should only be made by those who understand the reasoning behind existing structures.

## Key Insight
Things that seem pointless or stupid often exist for reasons that aren't immediately obvious. Before you remove or change something, understand what problem it was solving—otherwise you might reintroduce that problem or create worse ones.

## How to Apply
Before changing or removing something:
1. Ask "Why does this exist?"
2. Research the history and original reasoning
3. Identify what problem it solves
4. Only then decide if it should be changed
5. If you can't understand why it exists, don't change it yet

## Real-World Example
You join a company and see a seemingly pointless approval process for small purchases. You think "This is bureaucratic waste!" and eliminate it. Three months later, costs have exploded—employees are making expensive impulse purchases. The "pointless" fence prevented this problem.

A new manager sees developers spending time on code reviews and thinks "They should be writing more code instead!" and eliminates reviews. Code quality plummets, bugs multiply, customers leave. The fence existed to catch errors before they reached production.

Someone visits a farm and sees a fence in the middle of a field, blocking nothing. "Useless fence!" They tear it down. Next week, cattle that used to be kept in a different pasture trample crops. The fence was moved in anticipation of moving the cattle; it looked useless but had a reason.

Medication: A doctor sees a patient on multiple medications and thinks "Too many pills!" and removes some. Symptoms that were controlled come back. Each medication was treating something specific.

## Common Pitfalls
- Assuming everything old is wise (some fences really are useless)
- Using this as an excuse to never change anything
- Not recognizing when conditions have changed enough that old solutions no longer apply
