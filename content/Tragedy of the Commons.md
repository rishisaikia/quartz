## Definition
When individuals acting in their own self-interest deplete or spoil a shared resource, even though it's in everyone's long-term interest to preserve it. Rational individual behavior leads to collectively irrational outcomes.

## Key Insight
What's rational for each person is disastrous for the group. Each person benefits from using more of the common resource, but everyone doing this destroys it. This isn't about bad people—it's about bad incentive structures that reward individuals for harming the collective.

## How to Apply
Recognize the tragedy:
- "Is this a shared resource?"
- "Do individuals benefit from overuse?"
- "Does overuse harm everyone long-term?"
- "Are there incentives to preserve it?"

Prevent it:
- Private ownership (eliminate the commons)
- Regulation and enforcement
- Social norms and reputation
- Align individual and collective incentives

## Real-World Example
**Classic example - Grazing commons**: Medieval villages had shared pastures. Each farmer benefited from adding one more cow (individual gain), but too many cows overgrazed and ruined the pasture (collective loss). Each farmer thought "one more cow won't matter," but everyone thinking this destroyed the commons.

**Overfishing**: Oceans are a commons. Each fishing company benefits from catching more fish. But everyone overfishing depletes fish stocks, eventually collapsing the fishery and hurting everyone. Individually rational, collectively disastrous.

**Climate change**: The atmosphere is a commons. Each country/company benefits from cheap fossil fuels. But everyone doing this causes climate change that harms everyone. Each actor's contribution seems negligible, but aggregate effect is catastrophic.

**Office kitchen**: Office snacks are a commons. Each person taking "just one more" cookie is rational. But if everyone does this, snacks disappear and must be restocked constantly or run out, frustrating everyone.

**Open source software maintenance**: Popular open source projects benefit everyone. But maintaining them is unpaid work. Everyone wants to use the software (benefit from commons) but few contribute to maintenance (preserve the commons). Project quality degrades.

## Common Pitfalls
- Blaming individuals when it's a structural problem
- Expecting voluntary restraint to solve the problem (it rarely does)
- Not recognizing commons situations in your own life
- Assuming tragedy is inevitable (it's not—good design prevents it)
- Ignoring the power of social norms in small groups to prevent tragedy
