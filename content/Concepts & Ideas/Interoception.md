Locked inside the skull, our body is like any other external stimuli source for our brain. And any interoceptive cue (a dull ache in the tummy) could have many possible causes. Our body needs to make sense of these cues, and it does so by prediction. Usually, we experience interoception in general terms (feelings of pleasure, displeasure, arousal, calmness), but sometimes we experience moments of intense interoceptive sensations as emotions. 

In other words, interoception is the stimulus that the brain perceives from the inner body, and the interoceptive network is the organ that does this for us. The network has 2 distinct roles:
1. Body budgeting: sends predicttions to the body to control internal environment (e.g. speed up heart beat, slow down breathing, etc.)
2. Primary Interoceptive Cortex: represents sensations inside your body.

Every time the body budgeting region predicts any change (e.g. increase heart beat), it also predicts the sensory consequences of that change (e.g. pounding feeling). These are called interoceptive predictions, and they flow to the PIC, where they are simulated in the usual way. The PIC also receives inputs from other parts of the body - it matches these signals with the simulations it receives from the BB region. 

For e.g. suppose you see your boss. Since you believe that she judges your every move, your BB region makes a withdrawal (i.e. it predicts that you will need energy, so releases cortisol, which results in pumping glucose into your body). This is accompanied by a surge in interoceptive sensations. 

So interoception is just about perceiving the stimulus, not how it is interpreted. Interoception is perceived as affect, which is interpreted using concepts, which in turn is experienced as emotion.
