Instead of our visual senses being a mirror of the external world, they are heavily dependent on  *inference*. 

This is established by the following:

1. **Impossible objects**: The existence of impossible objects tells us:
	1. *Mind as Mirror*: The mind as mirror metaphor cannot be right since we cannot hold an accurate representation of an impossible object in our minds. 
	2. *Perceiving in Instalments*: We grasp different parts of an object at different times. Since scanning various parts of impossible objects makes us feel it's 3D and 2D, we perceive objects in parts. 
	3. *Confidence*: When looking at an impossible object, we have confidence that we're perceiving a 3D object; however, this confidence is misplaced because an impossible object cannot exist in the real world. 
2. **Sparseness of our perception**: Our eyes are constructed in such a way that only a small area around the fovea has enough sensitivity to perceive both granular detail and rich colour. Anything beyond this area is a gray fuzz; however, our mind tricks us into thinking that the entire visual spectrum is steady and available to us in rich detail. One way to look beyond this trick and realise the narrow focus of our eyes is the following optical illusion where the dark spots seem to appear only in the areas where we are directing our gaze. 
3. **Saccades**: Further, our eyes move rapidly in saccades every second, yet we perceive the images to be stable. Our eyes jump from point to point on a scene, and stitch together an illusion of rich visual world. This jumping or *jitter* is partly unconscious. Retinal stabilisation helps look beyond this.
