## Definition
Populations change over time through variation, selection, and retention. Traits that increase survival and reproduction become more common; traits that decrease them disappear. This process explains not just biology, but business, culture, and ideas.

## Key Insight
Evolution doesn't have a plan or purpose—it's simply "what survives, persists." Random variations occur; some help survival, some hurt it, most don't matter. Over time, helpful traits accumulate not because of design, but because organisms without them died. This process applies far beyond biology.

## How to Apply
Think evolutionarily:
- Generate variety (try many things)
- Select what works (ruthlessly cut what doesn't)
- Retain and build on winners (double down on success)
- Repeat the cycle

Recognize evolutionary processes:
- Why does this practice/business/idea exist? (Because it survived)
- What's the selection pressure? (What determines survival?)
- What variations are being tested?

## Real-World Example
**In biology**: Giraffes don't have long necks because they "decided" to reach high leaves. Random mutations created variation in neck length. Giraffes with slightly longer necks got more food, survived better, had more offspring. Over millions of years, long necks were selected for. No plan, just variation and selection.

**In business**: Successful companies aren't necessarily well-designed—they're survivors. Thousands of startups try different approaches (variation). Most fail (selection). Successful ones persist and grow (retention). The business environment "evolves" through this process, not through central planning.

**In culture**: Why do we shake hands as a greeting? Not because someone designed it, but because it evolved. Many greeting rituals were tried; handshaking survived because it showed you weren't holding a weapon. Useful practices survive; useless ones disappear.

**In ideas**: Academic fields evolve. Scholars propose theories (variation), evidence and argument determine which survive (selection), successful theories get built upon (retention). Bad theories eventually die out not because they're "proven wrong" but because they're selected against.

## Common Pitfalls
- Thinking evolution implies progress or improvement (it doesn't—just survival)
- Assuming current forms are optimal (they're just what survived so far)
- Not recognizing evolutionary processes in non-biological domains
- Underestimating how powerful variation + selection is over time
- Trying to skip the variation phase and just "pick winners"
