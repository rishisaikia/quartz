## Definition
The minimum amount of something required to start or sustain a chain reaction or self-sustaining process. The tipping point where momentum becomes self-reinforcing.

## Key Insight
Many systems require a threshold level before they work at all. Below critical mass, efforts fizzle out. Above it, growth becomes self-sustaining. Understanding where this threshold lies determines whether to push harder or abandon the effort.

## How to Apply
Before starting a project, ask:
- What's the minimum viable threshold for this to work?
- Am I willing to invest enough to reach critical mass?
- What are the signs I'm approaching the tipping point?
- Is it worth pushing through the hard early phase?

## Real-World Example
A nightclub needs critical mass. If only 10 people show up, it feels empty and awkward—more people leave than arrive. But once it hits 100 people, it feels lively and attracts more people. The energy becomes self-sustaining. Smart club owners give free entry early to seed the critical mass.

Wikipedia needed critical mass of articles before it became useful. With only 100 articles, it wasn't worth visiting. With 100,000 articles, people started using it regularly, which attracted more contributors, creating more articles.

A new social app needs enough users before it's useful to anyone. Below critical mass, there's no one to interact with. Above it, it becomes the place everyone needs to be.

## Common Pitfalls
- Giving up right before reaching critical mass
- Overestimating how much is needed to reach the threshold
- Not recognizing when critical mass is impossible to reach
