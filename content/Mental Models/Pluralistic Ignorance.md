## Definition
A situation where most people privately reject a norm or belief, but they incorrectly assume everyone else accepts it. This leads people to go along with something no one actually supports.

## Key Insight
We often misread the room spectacularly. You assume others believe something because they're not objecting, but they're silent because they think YOU believe it. Everyone conforms to a norm that secretly, nobody supports. This maintains dysfunctional situations that could collapse if people just talked honestly.

## How to Apply
When you feel pressure to conform, ask:
- "Do people actually believe this, or are they just going along?"
- "What would happen if I voiced my actual opinion?"
- "Am I staying silent because of what others actually think, or what I assume they think?"

Breaking pluralistic ignorance often just requires one person to speak up.

## Real-World Example
College drinking culture: Students overestimate how much their peers enjoy binge drinking. Most students privately feel uncomfortable with the amount of drinking, but everyone goes along with it because they think everyone else loves it. Each person is performing for an audience that's also performing. When surveys reveal true private attitudes, the culture can shift rapidly.

Meetings where everyone stays silent: The boss proposes a bad idea. Everyone in the room thinks it's terrible, but no one speaks up because they assume others support it. The boss interprets silence as agreement and proceeds. Meanwhile, each person thinks "Why is no one else objecting? I must be wrong."

Fashion and trends: People wear uncomfortable clothing or follow trends they dislike because they think everyone else genuinely likes them. In reality, most people are just conforming to what they think others want.

Political correctness: People may privately hold moderate views but stay silent, assuming they're in the minority. This creates an illusion of consensus around more extreme positions that most people don't actually hold.

## Common Pitfalls
- Staying silent and perpetuating the very illusion that's constraining you
- Assuming you're the only one who feels differently
- Not testing your assumptions about what others believe
- Being the first to speak up can feel risky, but it often reveals widespread agreement with your "dissenting" view
