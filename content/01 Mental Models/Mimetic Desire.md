## Definition
We don't desire things independently—we desire what others desire. Our wants are copied from models (people we admire or compete with), creating rivalry and competition for the same objects.

## Key Insight
You think you want things for their inherent value, but mostly you want them because others want them. This creates endless dissatisfaction: once you get what your model has, you find a new model with new desires. Understanding mimetic desire reveals why wanting what others have never brings satisfaction.

## How to Apply
Recognize mimetic desire:
- "Do I want this, or do I want it because someone else has it?"
- "Who am I copying? Who's my model?"
- "Am I competing with someone for this?"

Break free:
- Choose desires deliberately, not by imitation
- Avoid models (people you compare yourself to)
- Want things for themselves, not for status

## Real-World Example
**Startups**: Founders often want to start companies because other founders they admire have startups—not because they have a burning problem to solve. They're imitating models (successful founders) rather than following genuine desire.

**Romantic rivalry**: You're interested in someone. Suddenly your friend is also interested. Your desire intensifies—not because the person changed, but because now you're in mimetic competition with your friend. The rival's desire increases your desire.

**Fashion**: No one independently decided they wanted ripped jeans. Someone influential wore them, others copied, and it became desirable. The desire is mimetic—copied from models, not inherent.

**College prestige**: Students don't want Harvard because of specific educational benefits. They want Harvard because everyone else wants Harvard. The desire is self-reinforcing—the more people want it, the more others want it.

**Social media influencers**: Showing their perfect life makes you want that life—mimetic desire at industrial scale. You didn't want that vacation/outfit/lifestyle until you saw someone else having it.

## Common Pitfalls
- Not recognizing that most of your desires are imitated
- Achieving your desires and feeling empty (because they were never really yours)
- Creating rivalry by competing for the same things as your models
- Thinking you're immune to mimetic desire (you're not)
- Confusing mimetic desire with aspiration (some imitation is healthy)
