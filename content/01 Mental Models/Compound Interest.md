## Definition
Small gains or losses accumulate over time, with each period's growth building on the previous period's total, creating exponential rather than linear growth.

## Key Insight
Einstein allegedly called compound interest "the eighth wonder of the world." Small differences in growth rates create enormous differences over time. This applies to money, skills, habits, and relationships.

## How to Apply
Ask yourself: "What small, consistent actions compound positively in my life?" Then ask: "What small negative habits are compounding against me?"

## Real-World Example
Two friends start investing at age 25. Sarah invests $200/month with 8% annual returns. Mike waits until 35 and invests $400/month at the same rate. By age 65, Sarah has $700,000 while Mike has only $375,000—despite investing less money overall. The extra 10 years of compounding made Sarah's earlier small contributions worth more than Mike's larger later ones.

This works beyond money: Reading 20 pages daily seems small, but compounds to 30+ books per year. Small daily improvements in a skill compound into expertise.

## Common Pitfalls
- Underestimating how long compound effects take to become visible
- Giving up before reaching the inflection point
- Forgetting that negative habits also compound
