## Definition
A prediction that causes itself to become true. By believing something will happen and acting on that belief, you make it happen—creating a feedback loop between expectation and reality.

## Key Insight
Your beliefs shape your actions, which shape outcomes, which confirm your beliefs. This creates a self-reinforcing cycle that makes predictions come true not because they were accurate, but because believing them changed behavior. Understanding this reveals how much power expectations have over reality.

## How to Apply
Recognize self-fulfilling prophecies:
- "Is this prediction causing the outcome?"
- "How do my expectations change my behavior?"
- "Am I creating what I'm predicting?"

Use them positively:
- Expect success (and act accordingly)
- Expect others to succeed (they often will)
- Challenge negative prophecies before they fulfill themselves

## Real-World Example
**Bank runs**: If people believe a bank will fail, they rush to withdraw money. The mass withdrawals cause the bank to fail—the belief created the reality. The bank might have been fine, but belief in failure made it true.

**Student performance**: Teacher expects certain students to be smart and others to be slow. She unconsciously gives more attention, encouragement, and time to "smart" students. They perform better, confirming her belief. The "slow" students get less support, perform worse, confirming that belief too. The expectations created the outcomes.

**Job interviews**: You expect to fail an interview, so you're anxious and tentative. This nervousness makes you perform poorly. You fail the interview, confirming "I'm bad at interviews." The belief caused the failure.

**Relationship doom**: You believe your partner will leave you, so you become clingy and jealous. This pushes them away. They leave, confirming "everyone leaves me." Your fear of abandonment created abandonment.

**Market bubbles and crashes**: Everyone believes prices will keep rising, so everyone buys, driving prices up, confirming the belief. Until belief shifts and everyone believes prices will fall, so everyone sells, causing a crash. Mass belief creates mass reality.

## Common Pitfalls
- Not recognizing when your expectations are shaping outcomes
- Mistaking self-fulfilling prophecies for accurate predictions
- Letting negative prophecies run unchallenged
- Not leveraging positive prophecies intentionally
- Assuming all prophecies are self-fulfilling (some predictions are just accurate)
