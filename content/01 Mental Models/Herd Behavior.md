## Definition
The tendency for individuals to follow and mimic the actions of a larger group, often overriding their own private information or judgment. Each person's decision to follow others makes the crowd appear even more authoritative, amplifying the cascade.

## Key Insight
Herding is individually rational but collectively dangerous. If everyone else is doing something, there's probably a reason—copying them is often a reasonable heuristic. But when enough people follow this heuristic, the crowd behavior becomes decoupled from reality. The crowd is now tracking the crowd, not underlying truth.

## How to Apply
Recognizing herd dynamics:
- Is the action driven by independent judgment or by observing others?
- What information do the followers actually have?
- Is there a feedback loop where following makes the behavior look more credible?

Guarding against it:
- Seek out independent sources of information before acting
- Ask: "What would I do if I couldn't see what others were doing?"
- Be especially skeptical of sudden surges in popularity

## Real-World Example
**Financial bubbles:** Investors see others buying an asset, assume they have good information, and buy too. Each new buyer validates the premise for the next. Eventually the price disconnects from any underlying value. When the herd reverses, it reverses fast.

**Bank runs:** Rumors of insolvency spread. People see others lining up to withdraw and join them—even if they had no initial reason to worry. The herd behavior creates the crisis it feared.

**Restaurant choice:** You're choosing between two empty restaurants and one with a queue. You join the queue. So does the next person. Soon the first restaurant has a long line and you're not sure if the crowd knows something or just herded.

**Research citation:** Influential early papers get cited more, making them more influential, driving more citations. The field's consensus can drift toward early-mover advantage rather than truth.

## Common Pitfalls
- Assuming the crowd has done the research (they may just be following the crowd before them)
- Being immune to herding yourself while recognizing it in others
- Contrarianism as the opposite error—automatically doing the opposite of the crowd
- Missing that some herd behavior is information: crowds are sometimes right
