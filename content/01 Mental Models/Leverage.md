## Definition
Using a small input to generate disproportionately large outputs through tools, systems, or other people's resources.

## Key Insight
"Give me a lever long enough and I'll move the world." The most successful people don't work harder—they work with better leverage. An hour of leveraged work can produce 100x the results of an hour of unleveraged work.

## How to Apply
Constantly ask: "How can I get more output from the same input?"
- Use technology (automation, software)
- Use other people's labor (delegation, hiring)
- Use capital (money working while you sleep)
- Use media (one-to-many communication)

## Real-World Example
A consultant makes $200/hour working one-on-one with clients. She's trading time for money with zero leverage—when she stops working, income stops. Then she creates an online course teaching the same material. She invests 100 hours creating it once, then sells it to 1,000 people for $200 each. That's $200,000 from the same 100 hours—but the work is done once and leveraged infinitely.

Similarly, a manager who learns to delegate effectively can oversee projects worth millions while a solo worker can only complete what they personally touch.

## Common Pitfalls
- Over-leveraging and losing control or taking on too much risk
- Trying to leverage everything (some things require hands-on work)
- Not investing in building leverage early enough
