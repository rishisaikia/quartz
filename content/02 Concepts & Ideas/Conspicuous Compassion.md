## Key Insight
Conspicuous Compassion or blatant benevolence (or Charity) is fundamentally a public activity. We might derive some psychological rewards for anonymous charitable acts, but by and large, we want to be seen being charitable. This is because we use charity to normalise the violation of the norm not to show off, it hence helps us signal to achieve higher status. It helps us display *wealth* and *prosocial orientation* to attract mates and allies. All of these make us attractive to potential mates and allies. 

This explain some of the traits of our charitable acts:
- **Visibility** - We give more when we are seen; we give more during door-to-door solicitations when there are 2 people asking. 
- **Peer Pressure** - We respond strongly to social pressures. People seldom initiate donations on their own.. Ultimately, it is a competition
- **Proximity** - We prefer to help people locally rather than globally.
- **Relatability** - We give more to people who are relatable. *Identifiable Victim Effect*. Eventually, we are catering to an audience, and if our beneficiaries aren't relatable to our audience, it doesn't make sense to be charitable.
- **Mating motive** - We are more generous when primed with a mating motive.

## Real-World Examples
Tesla works by giving us an excuse to make our charity conspicuous. It normalises the violation of the norm not to show off, and helps us signal to achieve higher status.
