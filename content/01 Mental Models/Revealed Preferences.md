## Definition
What people actually choose, given real options and real costs, reveals their true preferences more accurately than what they say they prefer. Revealed preferences are observable through behavior; stated preferences are what people report when asked.

## Key Insight
People's stated preferences are often unreliable—shaped by social desirability, self-deception, and the gap between abstract values and concrete choices. Actions, especially costly ones, reveal what people actually value. Economists trust revealed preferences over stated ones because behavior is harder to fake.

## How to Apply
Reading others:
- Watch what they do with scarce resources: time, money, attention
- "Show me your calendar and your bank statement—I'll tell you your values"
- Discount verbal commitments; weight behavioral patterns

Reading yourself:
- When your stated preferences conflict with your behavior, your behavior is more honest
- Ask: "If I had to bet money on what I'd choose, what would I predict?"

## Real-World Example
**Environmentalism:** People say they care about climate change. Many also fly frequently, eat meat, and drive large cars. The revealed preference is for personal convenience; the stated preference is for environmental protection. Both can be true—humans contain contradictions—but the behavior is more reliable data.

**Parenting choices:** Parents say education quality is their top priority for choosing where to live. But revealed preferences (home prices, commute tolerance, proximity to family) show many other factors dominate.

**Product research:** Customer surveys say users want more features. Users actually abandon products that are too complicated. The survey captured stated preference; churn data captured revealed preference.

**Politics:** Voters say they want honesty and policy substance. Campaign data reveals they respond to emotional narratives and tribal signaling. Politicians who act on revealed rather than stated preferences win more elections.

## Common Pitfalls
- Assuming stated and revealed preferences always diverge (sometimes people mean what they say)
- Using revealed preferences to dismiss all introspection as useless
- Ignoring that choice environments constrain revealed preferences—people choose from available options, not ideal ones
- Confusing revealed preferences with optimal preferences (people reveal mistakes too)
