## Definition
Continuing an endeavor because you've already invested time, money, or effort into it, even when continuing makes no rational sense.

## Key Insight
Past investments are gone. They should not influence future decisions. The only relevant question is: "Given where I am now, what's the best path forward?" But humans are wired to avoid "wasting" what we've already spent.

## How to Apply
When tempted to continue something because you've already invested:
- Ignore what you've already spent (it's gone either way)
- Ask: "If I were starting fresh today, would I choose this?"
- Ask: "What would I tell a friend in this situation?"

## Real-World Example
You've watched the first hour of a terrible movie. You think "I've already invested an hour, I should finish it to not waste that time." But the hour is gone whether you finish the movie or not. Watching another hour of a bad movie means wasting another hour—doubling your loss instead of cutting it.

In business: A company has spent $10 million developing a product that clearly won't succeed. Instead of stopping, they invest another $5 million because "we've come too far to quit now." The $10 million is gone. The question is whether the next $5 million is a good investment (it's not).

In relationships: Staying in an unhappy relationship because "we've been together for 5 years" wastes future years, not just the past 5.

## Common Pitfalls
- Overcorrecting and quitting everything at the first difficulty
- Not distinguishing between sunk costs and valuable learning
- Using this to justify giving up on worthwhile long-term goals
