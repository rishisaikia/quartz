Sex seems inefficient. Many organisms reproduce just fine without sex:

- Dandelions clone themselves from seeds
- Virgin greenflies give birth to daughters already pregnant with granddaughters
- Willow trees grow from cuttings

So why do nearly all complex animals—every mammal, bird, and most others—bother with sex?

## What Sex Actually Does

Sex involves two genetic processes:

**Recombination**: Chromosome pairs swap DNA chunks during meiosis, mixing genes from all four grandparents into each offspring

**Outcrossing**: Combining genetic material from two different parents

The result: Sex is really about **mixing genes**, not just reproduction.

## The Central Problem: Why Should Individuals Bother?

**The math doesn't add up.** Asexual reproduction has massive advantages:

- Twice as fast (every individual can reproduce, not just females)
- No energy wasted finding mates
- No males who don't give birth

This is called the **"cost of males"** or **"cost of meiosis"**. A single asexual female should quickly outnumber sexual populations.

Yet sex dominates. There must be a powerful individual advantage—but what?

## Failed Explanations

### "Sex drives evolution"

**The idea**: Genetic mixing creates variety for natural selection to work with, helping species evolve faster.

**The problem**: This treats evolution as a goal species pursue. But evolution is directionless—it makes some organisms more complex, others simpler, some unchanged. Evolution responds to problems; it's not an objective.

### "Sexual species share innovations faster"

**The idea**: Sexual populations pool beneficial mutations like open-source software. Rare good mutations can combine across individuals rather than waiting for both to appear in one asexual organism.

**The problem**: This benefits the _species_, not the _individual_. Natural selection is ruthless: a gene that helps the species but hurts the individual gets eliminated immediately, long before species-level benefits appear.

**Why individual incentives matter**: Evolution is primarily _within-species_ competition. A gazelle doesn't just outrun cheetahs—it must outrun other gazelles. Similarly, humans use intelligence not just to solve problems but to outwit, deceive, understand, and manipulate each other. Genes that sacrifice individual success for group benefit get wiped out.

### "Sex spreads good mutations"

**The idea**: Sex constantly shuffles genes into new combinations—like one giraffe ancestor inventing long legs while another invents a long neck, then sex combining both.

**The problem**: Benefits appear too slowly. By the time a good combination emerges generations later, asexual competitors have already taken over. Plus, if sex is good at _creating_ good combinations, it's equally good at _breaking them apart_.

## Promising Explanations

### Theory 1: Gene Repair

**How DNA repair works**: DNA's double helix has complementary base pairs (A with T, C with G). When one strand breaks, repair enzymes use the matching strand as a template.

**The problem**: When _both_ strands break at the same location, there's no template.

**Sex as solution**: Sex provides a fresh gene copy through:

- Recombination (from your other chromosome pair)
- Outcrossing (from another individual)

**Weakness**: If you just need backup copies, why not get them from close relatives instead of unrelated strangers?

### Theory 2: Muller's Ratchet (The Stronger Candidate)

**The accumulation problem**: Each generation accumulates ~100 random mutations from copying errors or radiation damage. Most (99%) are neutral, but harmful ones slowly pile up.

**Why asexual reproduction fails**: Imagine 10 water fleas where only 1 is mutation-free and only 5 survive each generation to breed:

- The perfect flea has a 50% chance of dying childless
- Once gone, recreating a defect-free flea requires multiple rare _reverse_ mutations (extremely unlikely)
- Meanwhile, fleas with 2+ defects are easily recreated by single mutations in already-flawed fleas

**The ratchet effect**: Like a ratchet that turns only one direction, the average mutation count inevitably increases. It's like making photocopies of photocopies—quality degrades irreversibly, and recovering the original becomes nearly impossible.

**Sex as solution**: Sexual reproduction constantly reshuffles genes. By combining the least-damaged chromosomes from two parents, sex can recreate defect-free combinations without waiting for reverse mutations. It's like having two degraded photocopies and piecing together the clean sections from each.

## The Remaining Question

Sex must provide immediate, tangible advantages to individuals that outweigh its enormous costs. The mutation-clearing hypothesis is compelling, but the full answer to why sex persists—called "the queen of problems" in evolutionary biology—remains partially unsolved.

**The paradox endures**: Selfish individuals _should_ abandon sex and dominate. That they don't suggests sex offers something so valuable at the individual level that it's worth cutting your reproductive rate in half.
