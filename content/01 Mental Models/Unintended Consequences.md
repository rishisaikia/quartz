## Definition
Outcomes of an action that were not foreseen or intended, often because of complex system interactions, delayed effects, or human responses to incentives.

## Key Insight
Complex systems don't react in simple, predictable ways. Every intervention creates ripple effects. People respond to your actions in ways you didn't anticipate. The cure can be worse than the disease. Good intentions don't guarantee good outcomes.

## How to Apply
Before implementing a solution:
- Ask: "And then what happens?"
- Consider: "How might people game this system?"
- Think: "What could go wrong?"
- Look for: "Who is affected that I haven't considered?"

Expect surprises and build in correction mechanisms.

## Real-World Example
Prohibition in 1920s America intended to reduce alcohol abuse and crime. Unintended consequences: massive increase in organized crime, dangerous bootleg alcohol causing deaths and blindness, prison overcrowding, and disrespect for law enforcement. The "solution" created worse problems than it solved.

In medicine: Antibiotics were miracle drugs. Unintended consequence: overuse created antibiotic-resistant superbugs that are now a major threat.

A city bans plastic bags to help the environment. Unintended consequences: Reusable bags require more resources to manufacture, people buy more garbage bags (which are thicker plastic), and if people don't wash reusable bags, food contamination increases.

Vietnam-era U.S. military measured success by enemy body counts. Unintended consequence: troops inflated numbers, including civilians, creating incentive to maximize deaths rather than achieve strategic objectives.

## Common Pitfalls
- Paralysis from fear of any unintended consequence
- Assuming all consequences can be foreseen (they can't)
- Not monitoring for unintended effects after implementation
