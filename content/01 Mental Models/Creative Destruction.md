## Definition
The process by which new innovations destroy old industries and business models while creating new ones. Progress requires destroying the old to make room for the new.

## Key Insight
Economic progress isn't about making existing things better—it's about replacing old things with fundamentally new things. This is painful for those invested in the old, but necessary for advancement. Fighting creative destruction usually fails; adapting to or leading it succeeds.

## How to Apply
Recognize creative destruction:
- "What new innovation threatens this industry?"
- "Is this improvement or replacement?"
- "Am I defending the old or building the new?"

Navigate it:
- Don't fight inevitable destruction
- Invest in the replacement, not the declining
- Be willing to destroy your own products (cannibalize yourself)

## Real-World Example
**Automobiles destroyed the horse and buggy industry**: Blacksmiths, stable owners, buggy manufacturers, hay suppliers—all destroyed by cars. This was painful for those industries but created massive new value (auto manufacturers, gas stations, mechanics, road construction).

**Digital photography destroyed Kodak**: Kodak invented digital cameras but fought the transition to protect film business. Meanwhile, digital photography creatively destroyed their entire industry. Kodak filed for bankruptcy. Creative destruction doesn't wait for permission.

**Streaming destroyed video rental**: Netflix destroyed Blockbuster by offering a better model. Blockbuster could see Netflix coming but couldn't adapt—they were too invested in stores and late fees. Netflix later destroyed its own DVD business by pivoting to streaming.

**Smartphones destroyed many industries**: Cameras, GPS devices, MP3 players, alarm clocks, calculators, portable gaming devices—all replaced by smartphones. Each was creatively destroyed, but smartphones created new industries (apps, mobile gaming, ride-sharing).

**Amazon destroying retail**: Brick-and-mortar retail being replaced by e-commerce. Painful for traditional retailers, but creates value through lower prices and convenience. Resisting didn't save Toys R Us or Borders.

## Common Pitfalls
- Fighting inevitable creative destruction (usually fails)
- Protecting dying business models instead of building new ones
- Not recognizing when you're being creatively destroyed
- Assuming your industry is immune to disruption
- Refusing to cannibalize your own products (someone else will)
