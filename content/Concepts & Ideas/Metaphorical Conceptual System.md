Our [[Goal-based concepts|conceptual systems]] - the way we perceive, understand and act in the world are metaphorical, i.e. we perceive abstract concepts by comparison with other known concepts. These underlying metaphors that greatly influence our perceived reality are not apparent, but we can start to identify them by looking at linguistic evidence - since our conceptual systems are metaphorical, the language we use is also metaphorical; hence, if you decode the language, you can decode the underlying conceptual metaphors

The Thousad Brains theory also suggests that we [[Our Brain Captures Knowledge in Reference Frames#^8017e4|learn concepts by preserving them in a reference frame]], similar to how we preserve our knowledge of physical objects or environments. It also suggests that conceptual knowledge also uses recursive knowledge, i.e. a reference frame for a particular concept could comprise of links to reference frames of other concepts. Basis this theory, thinking about these concepts is akin to us moving around these reference frames, much like moving around on a map (see [[Our Brain Captures Knowledge in Reference Frames#^2241ad|Method of Loci]]). Which is why spatial metaphors are so predominant for us.
