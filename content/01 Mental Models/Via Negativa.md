## Key Insight
We have a bias toward action and addition. "Don't just stand there, do something!" But often the best solution is "Don't just do something, stand there!" Removing what doesn't work is often more powerful than adding what might work.

## How to Apply
Before asking "What should I add?" ask:
- "What could I remove?"
- "What's causing harm that I could stop doing?"
- "What would happen if I did nothing?"

## Real-World Example
You're constantly tired and feeling unhealthy. The addition approach: Add exercise, supplements, meditation apps, new diet plans, productivity systems. The via negativa approach: Stop eating after 8pm, remove social media from your phone, stop drinking alcohol on weeknights, eliminate toxic relationships.

In medicine: A patient has 8 medications and feels terrible. Instead of adding a ninth medication to treat the side effects, a good doctor reviews whether some medications can be removed. Often subtracting medications improves health more than adding them.

## Common Pitfalls
- Becoming a minimalist extremist (some addition is good)
- Removing things without understanding why they existed
- Paralysis from fear of adding anything
