## Definition
The range of ideas and policies that are considered acceptable or mainstream in public discourse at any given time. Ideas outside this window are dismissed as radical, extreme, or unthinkable.

## Key Insight
Not all ideas are equally debatable. Some are inside the window (acceptable to discuss), some outside (dismissed as crazy). The window can shift—ideas that were unthinkable become radical, then acceptable, then policy. Understanding the Overton Window reveals how social change happens and how to shift it.

## How to Apply
Map the window:
- What's currently unthinkable?
- What's radical but discussed?
- What's acceptable mainstream?
- What's current policy?

Shift the window:
- Push boundaries to expand acceptable range
- Make the formerly radical seem reasonable by comparison
- Build on small shifts to create larger change

## Real-World Example
**Gay marriage**: In the 1990s, gay marriage was outside the Overton Window—unthinkable to most Americans. Activists pushed the window by first advocating civil unions (radical but discussable). This made gay marriage seem less extreme by comparison. Over 20 years, civil unions became acceptable, then gay marriage became acceptable, then it became policy. The window shifted.

**Drug policy**: Marijuana legalization was unthinkable in the 1990s, radical in the 2000s, acceptable in the 2010s, and now policy in many states. The Overton Window shifted through persistent advocacy and changing norms.

**Socialism in America**: "Socialist" was a political death sentence in 2010. By 2020, openly socialist politicians were mainstream, and policies like universal healthcare were inside the window. The window shifted rapidly.

**Advocating extreme positions**: Activists sometimes advocate for positions beyond what they actually want, to shift the window. By making extreme positions visible, moderate positions seem reasonable by comparison. Example: "Defund the police" makes "reform the police" seem moderate.

**Corporate acceptance**: Work-from-home was outside the Overton Window for most companies pre-COVID. The pandemic forced it into policy overnight, shifting the window permanently for many organizations.

## Common Pitfalls
- Assuming the current window is natural or permanent
- Not recognizing when the window is shifting
- Advocating outside the window without strategy to shift it first
- Mistaking what's inside the window for what's true or right
- Not realizing you can actively work to shift the window
