## Definition
We discount future rewards much more steeply than rational calculation suggests, and our discount rate decreases over time. We're heavily biased toward immediate rewards—but our future selves aren't. This creates predictable inconsistency: we make plans we then don't follow.

## Key Insight
If you ask people "would you prefer $50 today or $60 next week?", many choose $50 now. But if you ask "would you prefer $50 in 52 weeks or $60 in 53 weeks?", most choose $60 in 53 weeks. The delay is the same, but the proximity to the present changes everything. We're not consistently impatient—we're hyperbolically impatient about the near future specifically.

## How to Apply
Designing for your future self:
- Pre-commit before the temptation arrives (sign up for the gym before motivation fades)
- Increase friction for immediate temptations; reduce friction for long-term goals
- Make future consequences more vivid and proximate to counteract discounting

Recognizing the pattern:
- "I'll start tomorrow" is almost always hyperbolic discounting
- Plans that require future-you to be more disciplined than present-you fail
- The gap between stated intention and actual behavior often traces to this bias

## Real-World Example
**Exercise:** People join gyms in January with genuine intention to go regularly. By March, they don't go. It's not that they changed their preference for health—it's that each individual day, the immediate cost of going (effort, time) outweighs the distant benefit (health in 10 years). The preference is hyperbolic.

**Savings:** People know they should save more for retirement. When they get a raise, they spend it. Automatic enrollment with gradually increasing contributions works because it bypasses the present-bias—you never feel the sacrifice.

**Deadlines:** Students with access to self-imposed binding deadlines perform better than those with only final deadlines. Pre-commitment devices work because they override future hyperbolic discounting.

## Common Pitfalls
- Moralizing about "lack of willpower"—the problem is structural, not character
- Designing systems that require consistent long-run discipline without pre-commitment
- Underestimating how differently you'll feel about a choice when it becomes immediate
- Ignoring that some present-bias is rational (the future is genuinely uncertain)
