## Definition
Understanding the boundaries of your own knowledge and expertise, operating primarily within those boundaries, and being honest about what lies outside them.

## Key Insight
You don't need to be an expert in everything. Extraordinary results come from knowing exactly where your knowledge is reliable and where it isn't. The size of your circle matters less than knowing its boundaries.

## How to Apply
For any decision or domain, honestly assess:
- Where do I have deep, reliable knowledge?
- Where am I operating on assumptions or superficial understanding?
- Should I stay within my circle, expand it, or get help?

## Real-World Example
Warren Buffett passed on investing in technology companies during the dot-com boom, even as many criticized him for missing out. He said he didn't understand the business models well enough. While others made fortunes (briefly), Buffett avoided massive losses when the bubble burst. He stayed within his circle of competence—businesses he deeply understood like insurance, consumer goods, and banking—and became one of the world's wealthiest people.

Conversely, people who made money in real estate often lose it trying stocks, or vice versa, because they mistake success in one domain for general expertise.

## Common Pitfalls
- Never expanding your circle (stagnation)
- Confusing confidence with competence
- Not seeking experts when outside your circle
