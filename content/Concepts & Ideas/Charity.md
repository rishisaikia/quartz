Charity helps us display *wealth* and *prosocial orientation* to attract mates and allies. It also helps us advertise our *spontaneous*, almost *involuntary* drive to help others. All of these make us attractive to potential mates and allies. 

All of these explain some of the traits of our charitable acts:
- Visibility - We give more when we are seen; we give more during door-to-door solicitations when there are 2 people asking. 
- Peer Pressure - We respond strongly to social pressures. People seldom initiate donations on their own.. Ultimately, it is a competition
- Proximity - We prefer to help people locally rather than globally.
- Relatability - We give more to people who are relatable. *Identifiable Victime Effect*. Eventually, we are catering to an audience, and if our beneficiaries aren't relatable to our audience, it doesn't make sense to be charitable.
- Mating motive - We are more generous when primed with a mating motive.

In light of the above, we know that we might derive some psychological rewards for anonymous donations, but by and large, we want to be seen. This is called Conspicuous Compassion or blatant benevolence. 

Tesla works by giving us an excuse to make our charity conspicuous. It normalises the violation of the norm not to show off, and helps us signal to achieve higher status.
