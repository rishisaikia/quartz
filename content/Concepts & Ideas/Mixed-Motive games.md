Old theory says we use self-deception as a defence mechanism because "we can't handle the truth!" So we filter out inconvenient truths to make life more tolerable. But this doesn't seem to make sense from an evolutionary perspective. If anxiety alleviation was the purpose, then evolution would have just made our brains more resilient, rather than doing this in such a roundabout way. 

New theory says we use self-deception to manipulate others. Since faking is effort intensive, and since we're always being judged by people who have partial view of our brains, the best way to deceive others is to believe our own lies first. This is clear in Mixed-Motive games where two parties have interests that overlap but also some interests that diverge. The overlap incentivises cooperation, but the divergent interests also encourages deception. These perverse incentives of mixed-motive games lead to option-limiting and other irrational strategies. 

- Closing or degrading a channel of communication
- Opening oneself to future punishment
- Ignoring information
- Purposefully believing something that's false

In other words, mixed-motive games contain incentives that encourage self-deception. This is contradictory to classical decision theory that says there's no value in sabotaging oneself. The idea is not to self-sabotage, but to convince other players that you have self-sabotaged. The value lies in how the self-sabotage impacts how others act. Hence, it is critical to self-deceive, else will be at a disadvantage.

*Why do we believe our own lies?*
Lying is hard to pull off since it's cognitively demanding and also we have to overcome the fear of getting caight. 

*Four Archetypes of Self-Deception:*

- **The Madman** - *"I am doing that no matter what!"* INTIMIDATE
When we fully and publicly commit to a certain course of action, it impacts how other players play the game. Which is why athletes play the madman by showing no signs of weakness. Nixon tried it during the Vietnam war.

- **The Loyalist** - *"Sure I will go along with your beliefs!"* EARN TRUST
Loyalty/ Belief is a political act. With unwavering belief and faith, one can impact the way other players view you. But loyalty requires a certain amount of irrationality. It's only called loyalty when the evidence suggests a difference course of action. When you continue in a company because it pays you double the salary, that's. not loyalty. When you stick around despite shitty pay, that's loyalty. "*The Truth is a poor litmus test of loyalty*"

- **The Cheerleader** - *"I know this is true. Come on, believe with me!"* CHANGE BELIEFS
Sometimes it is beneficial to be wrong in such a way that if everyone else believed the incorrect thing one believes in, one would be strategically better off. So cheerleading is about changing people's beliefs. And the more fervently we believe something, the easier it is to convince others that it's true. 

- **The Cheater** - *"My motives were pure. I have no idea what you are talking about!"* THROW OFF TRAIL
Many norms hinge on actor's intentions. When we decieve ourselves about our own motives, it becomes much harder for others to proscecute minor transgressions. Hence we often come up with [[Self Deception - Lying to Ourselves]] that paint us in a more flattering light.

*What does Self-Deception cost us?*
Cost of self-deception is sub-optimal decision-making. But we don't have to pay full-price. A part of our brain continues to know the truth. This leads to inconsistency. The brain is made up of a lot of different 'modules', each responsible for different information-processing tasks. Some modules handle low-level tasks like detecting edges in visual field, some handle medium-sized tasks like walking and speech, and some handle high-level tasks like detecting cheaters and managing our social impressions. Essentially each module is connected to each other, but also somewhat isolated. This is called [[Modularity]]. But more importantly, the different parts don't always agree. This is illustrated in the phenomenon called *blindsight*. People who have this condition swear they are blind, but when presented with flashcards, they guess the contents with a higher degree of accuracy than chance would let them. How all of this is relevant is that it is possible for our brain to *evaluate potential actions* while leaving them concealed from the parts of the brain that are responsible for *managing social impressions*. 

**Self Discretion**
It is about being discreet with potentially damaging information so as to protect our self image, and hence social image. When we spend more time and attention dwelling positive, self-flattering information, and less time and attention on shameful information, that's self-discretion.
