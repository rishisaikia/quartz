### Light Years

The Speed of Light is ***3 lakh km per second or 3 x 10$^5$ km per second***. Since 1 Light Year is the distance travelled by light in a year, i.e. 365 days. it follows that:

$$
\LARGE
1\;Light\;Year\;(LY)
$$
$$
\LARGE
= 3\;\times\;10^5\;\times\;365\;days\;\times\;24\;hours\;\times\;60 \;minutes \;\times \;60\; seconds\;
$$
$$
\LARGE
= 10\; trillion \;kms
$$
The nearest star to us (*Proxima Centauri*) 4.3 Light Years away. Hence, the distance in kilometres would be: 
$$
\LARGE
4\; Light \;Years\;=\;40\;trillion\;kms
$$

Similarly, Orion Nebula is 1400 LY away. Hence, distance in kilometres would be:
$$
\LARGE
1400\; Light \;Years\;=\;1400\;trillion\;kms\;!
$$

### Earth, Moon and Sun

>[!Info] Earth and Moon
>- Earth’s diameter: **13000 km**
>- Moon's diameter is about $1/4$ of Earth's; so around **4000 km**
>- Moon’s distance from Earth is 30 times earth’s diameter or **400,000 km**


![[moon-earth.png]]

So, given the speed and distance, time taken by light from moon to reach Earth would be:

$$
\LARGE
400,000\; km\;/\;300,000\;km\;per\;second=1.3\;seconds
$$
This is why there was a delay of around 3 seconds between the time Mission control and Apollo fights communicated.

- Sun's diameter: 1.5 Million km or 115 times that of Earth
- Sun's distance from Earth is around 150 Million km, or about 10,000 Earths or 100 Suns
 
Hence, time taken by light from Sun to reach Earth would be:

$$
\LARGE
150\;\times 10^6\;km\;/\;300,000\;km\;per\;second=8\;seconds
$$

When you look up at the star-filled sky on a clear night, all the stars visible to the unaided eye are part of the single collection of starts we call the Milky Way Galaxy. 

### Size of the Milky Way

The Sun is one of hundreds of billions of starts that make up the Galaxy, and is roughly 30,000 light years away from the centre. Hence from our position, we cannot see through the Milky Way's far rim because the space between the stars is filled with interstellar dust (small solid particles) and gas (mostly Hydrogen). 

>[!info] Density of Space
>*The air we breathe has about 10^19 atoms in every cubic cm. In space, 1 cubic cm has about 1 atom. Even our most familiar solids are mostly empty space. For example, every atom consists of a central positively charged nucleus surrounded by a negatively charged electron(s). The bulk of the matter in each atom is in the nucleus. The distance between the nucleus to its electrons is typically 100,000 times the size of the nucleus itself. For comparison, the distance from Earth to the Sun is only 100 times the size of the Sun.* 

Generally, the interstellar material is extremely sparse - it is a much better vacuum than anything we can produce in laboratories on Earth. Yet, the dust, building up over thousands of light years can block the light of more distant stars. To get a sense of the distribution of stars and the expanse of the Milky Way:

Within a **sphere centred on the Sun and with a radius of:
- **10 LYs**, we find **~10 stars**. 
- **100 LYs**, there are **~10,000 stars**. 
- **1000 LY**, we find **~10 MM stars**. 
- **100,000 LYs**, we finally encompass the entire Milky Way Galaxy with **billions of stars**.

For easy reference, astronomers have divided the sky into [[Modern Constellations| 88 different sections]], each named after a prominent star pattern within it.

### Structure of the Universe

From the Milky Way upwards and outwards, here are some more astronomical bodies and structures:
1. **Near Galaxies**: There are 3 small galaxies that are gravitationally bound to the Milky Way, and hence called satellite galaxies:
	1. The nearest galaxy to us, discovered in 1993, is a small dwarf galaxy (Sagittarius dSph) 80,000 LYs away in the Constellation of Sagittarius. 
	2. Two other galaxies are the LMC and SMC. Both were discovered by the Portuguese explorer Magellan during his circumnavigation of the earth, and hence named after him. 
		1. The Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC) is about 160,000 light years away. Visible from the Southern Hemisphere
		2. The Small Magellanic Cloud (SMC) is around 200,000 light years away. Visible from the Southern Hemisphere

	2. **Nearest Large Galaxy**: The nearest large galaxy is Andromeda (M31) about 2 MM LYs away. 

2. **Local Group:** A total of 54 such galaxies, along with the Milky Way, make up the Local Group. The Local Group has a diameter of 10 MM light years.
3. **Virgo Supercluster:** The Local Group, along with similar groups and clusters, are part of the Virgo Supercluster, which has a diameter of 110 M LYs. Estimates on number of galaxies within the Virgo Supercluster range from 1000 to 4000.  
4. **Lanieka Supercluster:** The Virgo Supercluster is part of an even larger cosmic structure known as the Laniakea Supercluster, which is a vast region of space that encompasses numerous galaxy clusters, galaxy groups, and superclusters, including the Virgo Supercluster. The name "*Laniakea*" comes from the Hawaiian language, meaning "immense heaven" or "immeasurable heaven." The Laniakea Supercluster was defined in a 2014 study by astronomers, who mapped the distribution of galaxies and determined the gravitational interactions that define this supercluster. It stretches over a tremendous scale, estimated to be about 520 million light-years in diameter.
5. **Cosmic Web:** The Laniakea Supercluster does not have a clearly defined larger structure or hierarchy that it is known to be a part of; however, the current understanding is that the Laniakea Supercluster represents a large-scale filamentary structure, with galaxy clusters and superclusters arranged along interconnected cosmic filaments. These filaments are part of the cosmic web, a complex network of structures that span vast distances in the universe.
