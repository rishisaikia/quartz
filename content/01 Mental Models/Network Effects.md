## Definition
A phenomenon where a product or service becomes more valuable as more people use it, creating a self-reinforcing cycle of growth.

## Key Insight
Most products decrease in value when more people use them (congestion, scarcity). But some become MORE valuable with each additional user. This creates winner-take-all dynamics and powerful competitive moats.

## How to Apply
When evaluating businesses or choosing platforms:
- Does this become better with more users?
- Are users creating value for each other?
- Is there a tipping point where growth becomes self-sustaining?

## Real-World Example
Telephones had weak network effects early on. If only 10 people have phones, each phone can only call 9 others—not very useful. But when millions have phones, each phone can reach millions. The value grew exponentially with each new user.

Social media works the same way: Facebook with 10 users is useless. Facebook with 3 billion users is where all your friends are, making it nearly impossible for competitors to dislodge. This is why Facebook could buy Instagram and WhatsApp for billions—they were building competing networks.

Email has network effects, but they're weaker (you can email across platforms), which is why no single company dominates email.

## Common Pitfalls
- Assuming all businesses can create network effects
- Underestimating how long it takes to reach critical mass
- Ignoring negative network effects (too many users degrading experience)
