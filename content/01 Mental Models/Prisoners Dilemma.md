## Definition
A situation where two individuals acting in their own self-interest produce a worse outcome than if they had cooperated. Mutual betrayal is the rational choice, even though mutual cooperation is better for both.

## Key Insight
Individual rationality doesn't always lead to collective rationality. When you can't trust the other person to cooperate, betraying them is safer—even though both of you betraying each other makes you both worse off than if you'd both cooperated. This explains why cooperation is hard and why trust matters.

## How to Apply
Recognize prisoner's dilemmas:
- "Would we both be better off cooperating?"
- "Do we each have incentive to betray?"
- "Is mutual betrayal worse than mutual cooperation?"

Solve them:
- Build trust and reputation
- Create repeated interactions (makes cooperation rational)
- Change incentives through contracts or enforcement
- Communicate and commit credibly

## Real-World Example
**Classic prisoner's dilemma**: Two criminals are arrested. Police separate them and offer each a deal: "Betray your partner and go free while they get 10 years. Stay silent and if they also stay silent, you both get 1 year. But if you both betray each other, you both get 5 years."

Best outcome: Both stay silent (1 year each). But each fears the other will betray, so both betray (5 years each). Rational individual choice leads to worse collective outcome.

**Business price wars**: Two companies would both profit from keeping prices high. But each is tempted to undercut the other to gain market share. Both drop prices, both lose profit. They'd be better off cooperating on high prices, but individual incentive is to betray.

**Arms races**: Two countries would both be safer spending less on weapons. But each fears the other will build more weapons, so both build massive arsenals at great cost. Both would be better off with mutual disarmament, but individual incentive is to arm up.

**Environmental regulations**: Companies would all benefit from clean air and water long-term. But each company benefits from polluting (lower costs). If one company follows expensive environmental rules while others don't, they lose competitive advantage. Individual incentive is to pollute even though everyone polluting is worst for all.

## Common Pitfalls
- Assuming cooperation will naturally emerge (it often doesn't)
- Not recognizing prisoner's dilemmas in daily life
- Betraying when cooperation would serve you better long-term
- Not building systems to make cooperation rational
- Ignoring the power of repeated games to enable cooperation
