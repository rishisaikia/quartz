## Definition
Building in a buffer between what you need and what you have to account for uncertainty, error, and bad luck.

## Key Insight
Things rarely go according to plan. A margin of safety protects you from the unexpected. It's the difference between a system that fails catastrophically at the first problem and one that handles surprises gracefully.

## How to Apply
In any plan, ask:
- "What if I'm wrong about my assumptions?"
- "What buffer would keep me safe if things go poorly?"
- "How much cushion do I need to sleep well at night?"

Then build in that margin deliberately.

## Real-World Example
A bridge engineer calculates that a bridge can safely hold 10 tons. But she doesn't rate it for 10 tons—she rates it for 5 tons. This margin of safety accounts for calculation errors, material defects, unexpected stress, and deterioration over time.

Financially: If you need $4,000/month to live, don't quit your job when you're making exactly $4,000/month freelancing. Wait until you're consistently making $6,000-7,000. The margin protects against slow months, unexpected expenses, and gives you breathing room to make good decisions rather than desperate ones.

## Common Pitfalls
- Building such large margins that you never take action
- Assuming margins eliminate all risk (they don't)
- Not adjusting margins as you gain competence
