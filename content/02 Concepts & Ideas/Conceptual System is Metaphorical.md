## Key Insight

In the book [[Metaphors We Live By]], Lakoff and Johnson propose a conceptual metaphor theory, which fundamentally challenges the idea that metaphor is just decorative language. Their argument is that our abstract reasoning is _necessarily_ metaphorical because we understand abstract domains by mapping them onto concrete, embodied experiences. **We can't directly grasp abstractions like time, argument, love, or ideas—so our brains automatically structure them using physical and spatial concepts we understand from bodily experience.**

These aren't conscious literary choices. They're cognitive structures operating below awareness, shaping how we perceive and reason about the world.

Further, most people think they reason logically and objectively, then sometimes use metaphors to communicate. Lakoff and Johnson invert this: **we reason _through_ metaphors, and our logic follows from whichever metaphors structure our thought. The metaphors are primary; the logic is derivative. This means changing how we think about something often requires changing the underlying metaphor**—which is difficult because these metaphors feel like reality itself, not like interpretive frames we've imposed.

## Why this matters philosophically

**Reality construction**: If our conceptual systems are metaphorical, then we're not simply describing an objective reality—we're partially _constructing_ our experienced reality through the metaphors we use. Different metaphors literally create different worlds of experience.

**Hidden constraints**: These metaphors constrain what we can easily think and what remains difficult or invisible. If ARGUMENT IS WAR, then collaborative truth-seeking becomes harder to conceptualize. If TIME IS MONEY, then the idea of "spending time" without productive output feels wasteful.

**Embodied cognition**: The theory suggests abstract thought isn't separate from bodily experience—it's grounded in it. We understand "grasping an idea" because we've physically grasped objects. We understand "warm feelings" and "cold logic" through temperature. "Looking forward" to the future reflects our physical orientation in space.

## How conceptual metaphors work

Take **ARGUMENT IS WAR**:

The linguistic evidence is everywhere:

- "Your claims are _indefensible_"
- "He _attacked_ every weak point in my argument"
- "I _demolished_ his argument"
- "She _shot down_ all my points"
- "If you use that _strategy_, he'll _wipe you out_"

But notice: we don't just _talk_ about arguments this way—we _experience_ them this way. We feel attacked. We strategize. We try to defend positions. We feel defeated or victorious. The metaphor structures the actual practice of argumentation in our culture.

Lakoff and Johnson point out this isn't inevitable. We _could_ conceptualize argument as dance—where the goal is coordinated movement, finding rhythm together, creating something beautiful. But we don't, and that shapes what arguments _are_ for us.

**TIME IS MONEY** is similarly pervasive:

- "You're _wasting_ my time"
- "That mistake _cost_ me an hour"
- "I don't have enough time to _spare_"
- "I've _invested_ a lot of time in this"
- "You need to _budget_ your time"

This metaphor makes time feel like a finite resource that can be spent, saved, or wasted. But this isn't universal—many cultures don't conceptualize time this way. The metaphor reflects and reinforces particular economic and cultural values.

**IDEAS ARE OBJECTS** / **MINDS ARE CONTAINERS**:

- "I can't _get_ that idea _into_ his head"
- "That concept is _hard to grasp_"
- "Let me _give_ you an idea"
- "Her mind is _full_ of strange notions"

**LOVE IS A JOURNEY**:

- "Our relationship is _at a crossroads_"
- "We're _stuck_ in this relationship"
- "It's been a _long, bumpy road_"
- "We may have to go our _separate ways_"

**THEORIES ARE BUILDINGS**:

- "The theory needs _support_"
- "The argument _collapsed_"
- "We need to _construct_ a solid framework"
- "That claim is _groundless_"

### Orientation metaphors

**GOOD IS UP / BAD IS DOWN**:

- "I'm feeling _up_ today" / "I'm feeling _down_"
- "Things are _looking up_" / "He fell _into_ depression"
- "That was a _high-quality_ performance" / "That's _beneath_ me"
- "_Top-notch_" / "_Low-class_"
- "She's at the _peak_ of her career" / "He _sank_ into despair"

**CONSCIOUS IS UP / UNCONSCIOUS IS DOWN**:

- "_Wake up_" / "_Fall asleep_"
- "I'm _up_ already" / "He _dropped off_ to sleep"
- "_Get up_" / "He _sank_ into a coma"

**HEALTH IS UP / SICKNESS IS DOWN**:

- "He's in _top_ shape" / "She came _down_ with the flu"
- "He's at the _peak_ of health" / "Her health is _declining_"
- "_Revive_, _rise_ from illness" / "_Fall_ ill"

**MORE IS UP / LESS IS DOWN**:

- "Prices are _rising_" / "The number _dropped_"
- "Turn the heat _up_" / "Turn it _down_"
- "_High_ expectations" / "_Low_ standards"

- "Inflation has _attacked_ the foundation of our economy"

### Particularly revealing ones

**VIRTUE IS UP / VICE IS DOWN**:

- "_High-minded_" / "_Low_ behavior"
- "_Upright_ citizen" / "_Fallen_ woman"

**RATIONAL IS UP / EMOTIONAL IS DOWN**:

- "_Higher_ faculties" / "_Base_ instincts"

- "What lies _ahead_?"

- "_Back_ in the old days"

### What this reveals

Notice how many of these cluster around similar source domains (UP/DOWN, CONTAINER, JOURNEY, FORCE, TEMPERATURE). This isn't random—these reflect fundamental embodied experiences: gravity and verticality, our bodies as containers, movement through space, physical causation, temperature sensation.

The patterns also reveal cultural values: the consistent mapping of GOOD/RATIONAL/CONSCIOUS/VIRTUE to UP suggests a deeply embedded hierarchy. The prevalence of MONEY and RESOURCE metaphors for abstract concepts (time, attention, energy) reflects economic thinking penetrating all domains of life.

Once you start noticing these, they're everywhere—and you realize how much of what feels like "literal" or "objective" description is actually metaphorical structuring we've naturalized so completely that it's become invisible.

## The neurological connection

**Our brains may be biased toward narrative and pattern-finding partly because metaphorical mapping is how we extend concrete reasoning to abstract domains.** The same neural systems that process physical space and movement get recruited for understanding time, relationships, and ideas. Mirror neurons, spatial processing in the parietal cortex, and sensorimotor systems all appear to activate even when we're thinking about abstract concepts—suggesting we're literally simulating physical experiences to understand abstractions.

## Other Notes

The [[Thousand Brains Theory]] theory also suggests that we [[Brain Captures Knowledge in Reference Frames#^8017e4|learn concepts by preserving them in a reference frame]], similar to how we preserve our knowledge of physical objects or environments. It also suggests that conceptual knowledge also uses recursive knowledge, i.e. a reference frame for a particular concept could comprise of links to reference frames of other concepts. Basis this theory, thinking about these concepts is akin to us moving around these reference frames, much like moving around on a map (see [[Brain Captures Knowledge in Reference Frames#^2241ad|Method of Loci]]). Which is why spatial metaphors are so predominant for us.
