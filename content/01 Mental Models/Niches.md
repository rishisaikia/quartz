## Definition
A specialized segment of a market or environment where specific adaptations allow an organism or business to thrive without direct competition. Finding and dominating a niche avoids head-to-head competition.

## Key Insight
You can't out-compete everyone at everything, but you can be the best at one specific thing in one specific context. Niches let specialists win where generalists can't. The riches are in the niches—narrow focus beats broad mediocrity.

## How to Apply
Finding your niche:
- "What specific problem do few others solve?"
- "Where is there demand but limited supply?"
- "What combination of skills/factors makes me unique?"
- "Who needs exactly what I offer?"

Dominating a niche:
- Go narrow and deep, not broad and shallow
- Own the specialty completely
- Expand only after dominating the niche

## Real-World Example
**In nature**: Hummingbirds evolved long beaks for specific flowers other birds can't access. They don't compete with other birds for general food—they dominate a niche. Similarly, giraffes eat leaves high in trees where other herbivores can't reach. Specialization into a niche reduces competition.

**In business**: Domino's doesn't try to be the best restaurant. They own the niche of "cheap pizza delivered fast." They lose to gourmet pizza places on quality, but dominate their specific niche.

**Instagram**: Launched when Facebook dominated social networking. Instead of competing directly, Instagram found a niche: photo-sharing on mobile. Dominated that narrow space, then expanded.

**Consultants**: Generic "business consultant" faces massive competition. "Supply chain consultant for medical device manufacturers in the Southeast" faces almost none. The narrow niche is easier to dominate and commands higher prices.

**Content creators**: "Fitness YouTuber" is crowded. "Fitness for people over 50 recovering from injuries" is a niche with less competition and more loyal audience.

## Common Pitfalls
- Choosing a niche that's too narrow (no customers)
- Choosing a niche that's too broad (too much competition)
- Not dominating the niche before expanding
- Assuming niches are permanent (they can get crowded or obsolete)
- Fighting to dominate a dying niche instead of finding a new one
